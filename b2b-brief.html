01 / Market

What the incumbents actually sell — and the gap that leaves

Competitive read

Both reference sites are the same business in different packaging. Kerix lists Moroccan companies and sells premium placement plus exportable contact files. Telecontact is the Moroccan Yellow Pages — free basic listings, paid visibility boosts, analytics upsell. Procurement.ma and Maroc Business sit adjacent, aggregating tenders and purchase requests.

Every one of them monetizes the seller's hope of being found. The seller pays whether or not a deal happens. The buyer pays nothing and is guaranteed nothing. Nobody in this market is paid for the transaction succeeding.

The actual insight

This isn't an untapped market — it's a mis-monetized one. The directories are lead-gen businesses that happen to look like infrastructure. The gap is the referee layer: verification, escrow, dispute resolution, and a reputation record that follows a company across deals. That's a different product with a different buyer and a different revenue line.

Worth naming plainly: "untalked-about" and "fresh" usually mean one of two things — nobody found it, or people tried and the economics didn't work. Before the next meeting, one of you should find out which. If a Moroccan B2B escrow or verified-supplier play has already died, the post-mortem is the most valuable document you'll read.

02 / Model

The PropFirmMatch comparison breaks where it matters most

Analogy audit

PropFirmMatch is a comparison and affiliate site. It ranks prop trading firms, takes commission on referred signups, and pays content creators ~10% to send traffic. It is a media business, not a referee. It never touches the money between the two parties and carries no liability if a firm behaves badly.

That distinction decides your entire architecture, so settle it at the meeting before anything else.

Model A — Comparison & lead-gen

What PropFirmMatch actually is. Rank suppliers, sell qualified leads or take referral commission.

  • Buildable in 6 months, plausibly less
  • SEO is genuinely the whole business
  • No regulatory exposure
  • Weak moat — copyable in a quarter
  • You compete directly with Kerix on their revenue line

Model B — Referee & escrow

What "middleman and referee" means literally. Verify both parties, hold funds, release on delivery, arbitrate disputes.

  • 12–18 months realistically, not 6
  • Handling third-party funds in Morocco is regulated — Bank Al-Maghrib territory
  • Needs a licensed payment partner (CMI, or a PSP) or a trust-account structure
  • Strong moat: transaction history compounds
  • This is the fundable, sellable version
Meeting decision — do not leave without it

Ask him directly: when you say referee, do we hold the money or not? If yes, legal and payment-partner research starts this month and the timeline doubles. If no, you're building a better Kerix and should say so out loud — that's a viable business, but it isn't a five-year exit at a price worth waiting for.

The pragmatic path is Model A as the wedge and Model B as the destination: launch as verified discovery and RFQ routing, earn the traffic and the supplier relationships, then add escrow once you have deal flow to justify the licensing work. That sequencing also matches how funding rounds want to see traction.

03 / Stack

WordPress for SEO: the argument that was true in 2014

Technical
disagreement

This is where I'd push back hardest, and where you have the strongest evidence on your side. "We need WordPress because we need SEO" was reasonable advice a decade ago. Today it's inherited folklore.

Google renders JavaScript. Next.js, Astro, SvelteKit and Nuxt all server-render or statically generate HTML — the crawler receives complete markup, exactly as it does from WordPress. WordPress's real SEO advantage was never the platform; it was Yoast making metadata editable by non-engineers. You can reproduce that with a CMS field and a sitemap route in about two days.

The compromise worth offering, if he holds firm: headless WordPress purely as the editorial CMS — blog, guides, landing copy — with the frontend rendered by your framework and the marketplace living in your own database. He keeps the editing experience he trusts, you keep one frontend and one performance profile. Concede the CMS, not the frontend.

How to run this argument

Don't argue theory. Pull up any Next.js site in a browser, view source, and show him the fully rendered HTML in the response. The claim collapses in about thirty seconds, and it collapses without anyone having to be wrong out loud.

04 / SEO

Right channel, wrong first move

Growth

His SEO instinct is correct and it's the strongest strategic idea in the plan. Moroccan B2B buyers search in French, intent is high, and Kerix's traffic proves the demand exists. Paid acquisition in this market is expensive relative to deal size. SEO is the right engine.

The aged-domain plan is the weak part. Buying a domain with high DR and clean backlinks is a real tactic, but it is a lottery ticket, not a strategy, and it comes with specific failure modes:

  • Topical mismatch. A domain whose backlinks point at an unrelated industry transfers far less authority than the DR number suggests. Relevance is the multiplier.
  • Hidden penalties. Manual actions don't show in third-party metrics. You inherit them silently.
  • DR is a vendor score. Ahrefs and Moz invented it. Google doesn't use it. It's directional, not predictive.
  • Reset risk. A wholesale change of topic and content can flush accumulated trust anyway.

Budget a few hundred dollars, buy one if a genuinely relevant Moroccan or francophone B2B domain surfaces, and treat any resulting head start as a bonus. Don't let the search for it block month one.

What actually wins this market is the thing incumbents structurally can't copy: programmatic pages backed by proprietary data. Kerix ranks on listings. You rank on listings plus verification status, response rates, completed-transaction counts, and sector pricing benchmarks. Every closed deal generates content nobody else can publish. That's a compounding moat, and it's the same asset that makes escrow possible later. It's also the single best reason not to put WordPress in the critical path.

05 / Funding

Build-then-fund is backwards for where you are

Sequencing

The plan is: build quietly, get funding through a country program, incorporate, scale, exit around year five. The sequencing works against you, because Morocco's current programs are designed to fund exactly the stage you're at right now.

As of 2026, Tamwilcom's Startup Venture Building program is deploying roughly MAD 700M (~$69M) across 800 startups over three years — founder stipends, prototype funding, loans up to MAD 2M. Separately, the Mohammed VI Investment Fund with CDG and the Ministry of Digital Transition has shortlisted managers to deploy about MAD 2.5B (~$270M) into early-stage companies under Digital Morocco 2030. These are ideation-and-prototype stage instruments, not growth rounds.

The cost of waiting

Building for six months unfunded means six months of runway from your own pockets, and you arrive at the program with a finished product asking for scale money — competing against companies asking for build money with a better story about what the funding unlocks. Applying at prototype stage is cheaper and strictly more likely to land.

There's a structural problem too: most of these instruments require a registered Moroccan entity. "No business until funding" and "get funding from a country program" are in direct conflict. Registering an SARL is inexpensive and fast; delaying it doesn't preserve optionality, it just makes you ineligible.

On the exit: a five-year horizon is a reasonable ambition but a bad plan, mostly because the Moroccan acquirer pool for a B2B marketplace is thin. Realistic buyers are regional players, a telecom or bank wanting SME distribution, or a pan-African expansion play. Each wants different things — one wants transaction volume, another wants verified-supplier data, a third wants the audience. That choice shapes what you instrument from day one, so it's worth a conversation now, not in year four.

06 / Scope

What six months actually buys

Estimate
audit

He's right that this isn't a weekend build, and right that it demands real engineering. But "at least 6 months" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. It's achievable for Model A in one vertical. It is not achievable for a full escrow platform with dispute resolution, KYB verification, and multilingual support.

Here's the honest decomposition — assume two people, part-time-to-full-time:

Model A lands around five to seven months of real work — his estimate is defensible. Model B is a year-plus. Both of you should agree on which number you're quoting before either of you commits to it in front of anyone else.

The cold-start problem nobody's costed

A marketplace with no suppliers is worthless to buyers, and vice versa. Kerix has 150,000 listings and a decade of head start. Your first hundred verified suppliers will come from phone calls and in-person visits, not from the product. That's months of unglamorous fieldwork that doesn't appear anywhere in an engineering estimate — and it's the single most common reason marketplaces like this die. Whoever isn't writing the escrow logic should be doing this.

07 / Split

Dividing the work without dividing the company badly

Roles

You asked how to frame the skills conversation. Don't split by technology — "you do frontend, I do backend" produces two people who each understand half a product. Split by outcome ownership, where each person owns a metric end to end.

Owner — Product & platform

  • Data model, taxonomy, search
  • Transaction and RFQ flows
  • Verification and trust systems
  • Infrastructure, security, deploys

Owns: does the core loop work and stay up.

Owner — Growth & supply

  • SEO architecture and content system
  • Supplier acquisition and onboarding
  • Funding applications, program relationships
  • Pricing and monetization

Owns: qualified traffic in, verified suppliers on.

Both people write code. Both talk to users. The split is about who is accountable when a number goes the wrong way — not about who is allowed to touch which files.

Say this at the meeting, awkward as it is

Equity, vesting, and what happens if one of you leaves. It's his idea and your execution — that tension resolves cheaply now and expensively later. A four-year vest with a one-year cliff is the standard answer and exists precisely so neither of you has to trust the other's future enthusiasm. Also agree who is CEO. Two-founder companies with no decision-maker stall on exactly the kind of disagreement you're about to have over WordPress.

09 / Next 30 days

What to do before writing any product code

Concrete
  1. Pick the vertical and call twenty suppliers in it. Not a survey — actual conversations. Ask how they currently find buyers and what a lost deal costs them. This validates or kills the whole thesis for the price of your time.
  2. Call ten buyers. Ask what goes wrong when they source from a new supplier. If "I got burned and had no recourse" comes up repeatedly, the referee model is real and you have your positioning line.
  3. Settle the escrow question, and if the answer is yes, book a call with a payments lawyer and one PSP. One week of research now saves a year of wrong architecture.
  4. Register the entity and check the eligibility criteria for the Tamwilcom program against your actual situation.
  5. Resolve the stack disagreement with the view-source demo, and write the decision down.
  6. Write a one-page memo together — model, vertical, monetization, who does what, equity. If you can't agree on one page, you won't agree on a codebase.
Bottom line

The market read is genuinely good and worth pursuing. Every serious risk in this plan is a sequencing risk, not an idea risk: building before validating, waiting on funding you could apply for now, and locking in a stack decision on a premise that stopped being true years ago. Fix the order of operations and the rest is ordinary hard work.
