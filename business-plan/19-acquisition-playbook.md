## 19. Acquisition playbook

**What this is:** the operational document for client acquisition. [§7](07-go-to-market.md) is the strategy; this is what you work from on a Tuesday morning. Scripts, list-building method, sequences, targets, and the weekly cadence.

**Status:** every script and target here is a **starting hypothesis**, not a finished asset. They are written to be used, measured, and rewritten. The first twenty calls will teach you more than this document contains.

---

### 19.1 The three channels at a glance

| | Channel | Target | Active | Success event | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CH1** | Supplier outbound | Suppliers | m1–8, tapering | Verified profile live | Growth |
| **CH2** | Buyer outbound | Buyers | m6–14 | **One real RFQ submitted** | Growth |
| **CH3** | SEO / organic | Mostly buyers | m9+, compounding | Inbound RFQ or supplier signup | Growth |

**§11.1 assigns all three to the Growth founder.** That is a real capacity constraint, not a formality — see §19.7.

### 19.2 Before anything: the legal position on cold outreach

**Read this before building a list.** `[ASSUMPTION — confirm with the same lawyer already booked for the escrow question in §9.3. This is one added question, not a separate engagement.]`

We will be registered with the CNDP and operating under Law 09-08 (§9.2). Three things to establish:

1. **"Publicly available" does not automatically mean "usable for marketing."** Data scraped from a directory or a public register is still personal data when it identifies a person (a named contact, a personal mobile, a nominative email). Company switchboard numbers and generic addresses (`contact@`) sit on safer ground than `nom.prenom@`.
2. **B2B outreach is generally treated more permissively than consumer marketing**, but the safe pattern is the same everywhere: identify yourself, state where you got the contact, offer an unambiguous opt-out in every message, and honour it immediately and permanently.
3. **Our own processing must be declared.** The F211 declaration (§9.2) should cover a prospect database from the start. Filing it is free and takes 24 hours — there is no reason to run outreach before it is done.

**Operational rules we adopt regardless of what the lawyer says:**

- **Maintain a suppression list from day one.** One "no" means never contacted again, on any channel, forever. This costs nothing to build now and is very expensive to retrofit.
- **Every email states where we got the contact.** "I found your listing on Kerix" is honest, disarming, and legally cleaner than pretending to a prior relationship.
- **No purchased lists.** Provenance we cannot explain is provenance we cannot defend.
- **Phone before email where a relationship matters.** Culturally stronger in this market and generates no written record we would be embarrassed by.

**One more reason to be careful.** We are selling *trust and verification*. A company that acquires customers through sloppy or borderline outreach is contradicting its own product in the first interaction. **The acquisition method is part of the trust proposition.**

### 19.3 CH1 — Supplier outbound

#### 19.3.1 Building the list

Sources, in order of quality:

| Source | Yield | Data quality | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Physical survey of the launch region** | High | Excellent | Materials depots are visible from the road. A day of driving produces names a directory never had |
| **Kerix** | Medium | Fair, stale | ~30,000 companies nationally `[VERIFIED — C]`. Construction-materials subset in one region may be low hundreds |
| **Telecontact** | Medium | Fair | Broader coverage, shallower B2B depth |
| **MABANI** | Low volume, high relevance | Good | Construction-specific; 1,600+ products, 60 categories. These suppliers are already digitally active |
| **Trade associations** | Low volume, very high quality | Excellent | An introduction converts several times better than a cold approach `[ASSUMPTION — identify the bodies]` |
| **Trade fairs** | High density | Excellent | One day, many conversations, warm context |
| **Referral from onboarded suppliers** | Compounds | Excellent | Incentivise with free verified months |

**Minimum viable record:** company name, city, category, phone, contact name if obtainable, source, date collected. **Record the source** — you will want to know which channel produced retained suppliers, not just signups.

**Target list size:** to reach 100 verified suppliers at the conversion rates in §19.3.4, build a list of **400–600** (matching §4.1's reachable estimate).

#### 19.3.2 Phase A — the interview script (months 1–2)

**This is not a sales call.** The rule from Appendix B governs: *ask, record, shut up.*

**Opening (phone, to book an in-person meeting):**

> « Bonjour, je m'appelle [nom]. Je fais une étude sur la façon dont les fournisseurs de matériaux trouvent leurs clients au Maroc. Je ne vends rien — je cherche à comprendre le marché avant de construire quoi que ce soit. Est-ce que je peux passer vous voir 30 minutes cette semaine ? »

**Why this wording:** "je ne vends rien" is true at this stage and it is the single most useful sentence in the script. It changes what people tell you. **If it stops being true, stop using it.**

**The interview itself:** use [Appendix B](18-appendices.md) verbatim. Eleven questions, in order, including the two added in v0.2 — the competitor question (are you on Kerix / Procurement.ma / BTPro / MABANI, did it produce an order?) and the settlement-delay question (would payment 1–2 days after confirmed delivery be better or worse than today?).

**Close every interview with the waiting-list ask:**

> « On construit quelque chose dans ce domaine. Est-ce que je peux revenir vers vous quand il y aura quelque chose à montrer ? »

**Record verbatim quotes, not summaries.** Gate 0 is assessed on what people actually said. A paraphrase cannot be checked and cannot be quoted to a funder.

#### 19.3.3 Phase B — the recruitment script (months 3–6)

Now you are selling. The draft pitch from §7.2, expanded into a call structure:

**1. Open with the re-contact (warm list) or the source (cold):**
> « On s'est parlé il y a quelques mois — vous m'aviez expliqué comment vous trouvez vos clients. On a construit la chose dont je vous avais parlé. »

Cold version:
> « J'ai trouvé votre entreprise sur [source]. Je vous appelle parce qu'on lance une plateforme pour les fournisseurs de matériaux dans la région de [région]. »

**2. The problem, in their words** — use what the interviews actually surfaced. If suppliers said unqualified enquiries waste their time, lead with that. **If the interviews contradicted our assumptions, this script is wrong and must be rewritten before use.**

**3. The offer:**
> « C'est gratuit pour être listé. Vous ne payez rien tant que ça ne vous ramène pas d'affaires. On vous vérifie une fois — documents, adresse — pour que les acheteurs sachent que vous êtes une entreprise réelle. Et vous n'entendez parler de nous que quand un acheteur avec un vrai projet demande un devis dans votre catégorie. »

**4. Remove the effort:**
> « Vous n'avez rien à remplir. Donnez-moi votre catalogue ou votre plaquette, je monte votre profil moi-même et je vous l'envoie pour validation. »

**Concierge onboarding is the conversion mechanism**, not a courtesy. A form is a reason to say "later"; "I'll do it for you" is not.

**5. The close:** a booked verification appointment, not a verbal yes.

**Objection handling:**

| They say | Respond with |
|---|---|
| "I already pay Kerix" | "What did it bring you? We only charge when you can see the enquiries" |
| "I have enough customers" | "This is for the weeks you don't. What happens in a slow month?" |
| "I don't have time" | "That's why I build the profile for you. Fifteen minutes, once" |
| "Is this like [competitor]?" | Be honest and specific about the difference — verification and recourse. **Never disparage; suppliers talk to each other** |
| "What does verified mean?" | Explain the actual checks (§10.1). **If this answer is vague, the whole product is vague** |

#### 19.3.4 CH1 targets and capacity

From §10.2's field-capacity figures:

| Activity | Time/unit | Units/week/person |
|---|---|---|
| Cold call → booked meeting | 20 min, ~15% conversion | ~40 calls |
| Site visit + pitch | 2 hrs incl. travel | 8–10 |
| Concierge onboarding | 1.5 hrs | 10–12 |
| Verification (Verified tier) | 1.5 hrs | 12–15 |

**One person doing field work full-time acquires ~6–8 verified suppliers/week.** 100 suppliers = **13–17 person-weeks** = three to four months of one founder's full time.

**This is the most commonly fatal omission in marketplace plans (§10.2) and it does not appear in any engineering estimate.** Whoever is not writing the RFQ logic is doing this.

| Month | Cumulative verified | Weekly rate needed |
|---|---|---|
| m3 | 10 | 2–3 |
| m4 | 25 | 4 |
| m5 | 40 | 4 |
| m6 | 60 | 5 |
| m8 | 70 | 3 (tapering) |
| m12 | 100 | 2 (inbound rising) |

**Leading indicator to watch weekly:** booked meetings, not signups. Signups lag by two weeks; an empty calendar this week is an empty pipeline next month.

### 19.4 CH2 — Buyer outbound (the bridge)

**This is the channel that keeps early suppliers from churning.** See [§7.3.1](07-go-to-market.md) for why it exists.

#### 19.4.1 Building the buyer list

| Source | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Ask every onboarded supplier: "which buyers do you wish would find you?"** | **Best source in the plan.** They know the market; a warm mention converts far better than cold |
| Public tender awards and contract records | Firms winning public works are actively buying materials, now |
| Sector press project announcements | New projects mean new procurement |
| Developer and construction-firm listings | Matches the 5–50 employee profile in §4.2 |
| LinkedIn — *achats* / procurement / *directeur travaux* roles | Named contacts at mid-size firms |
| Active sites in the launch region | Visible from the road; the site manager knows who supplies them |

**Minimum record:** firm name, contact name and role, phone, project type if known, source, date.

#### 19.4.2 The buyer approach

**The ask is one RFQ, not a signup.** This is the whole design of the channel.

**Phone script:**

> « Bonjour, je suis [nom] de [nom de la plateforme]. On travaille avec les fournisseurs de matériaux vérifiés dans la région de [région] — ciment, acier, agrégats. Vous avez un chantier en cours ? Si vous me dites ce dont vous avez besoin, je vous fais revenir trois devis de fournisseurs qu'on a vérifiés nous-mêmes. C'est gratuit, et vous n'avez rien à installer. »

**Why this works:**
- **No adoption cost.** No account, no software, no IT approval (§4.3 explicitly notes procurement officers will not pay for anything requiring IT approval)
- **The output is concrete** — three quotes, not "access to a platform"
- **It is genuinely useful even if they never return**, which is the correct posture for a trust product
- **"Fournisseurs qu'on a vérifiés nous-mêmes"** is the differentiator, stated in the first fifteen seconds

**Email version — short, and states provenance:**

> Objet : Trois devis pour votre chantier à [ville]
>
> Bonjour [nom],
>
> J'ai vu que [entreprise] travaille sur [projet/secteur] — j'ai trouvé vos coordonnées via [source].
>
> On a vérifié une centaine de fournisseurs de matériaux dans la région de [région] : documents, adresse, existence réelle. Si vous me dites ce dont vous avez besoin, je vous fais revenir trois devis comparables sous 48h. Gratuit, rien à installer.
>
> Si ça ne vous intéresse pas, répondez simplement « non » et je ne vous recontacterai pas.
>
> [nom, téléphone]

**The opt-out line is not optional** — see §19.2.

#### 19.4.3 CH2 targets

| Month | RFQs/month | Mostly from |
|---|---|---|
| m6 | 5 | CH2 entirely |
| m8 | 20 | CH2 |
| m10 | 40 | CH2, some organic |
| m12 | **80** (§10.4 target) | Mixed |
| m14 | 100+ | Organic overtaking |

**Quality bar:** §10.4 requires **>70% of RFQs receiving ≥3 quotes**. An RFQ that gets no quotes is worse than no RFQ — it teaches the buyer we don't work and burns a supplier's attention. **Route only what we can actually fill.** If a category has thin supply, say so and go get suppliers before soliciting demand in it.

#### 19.4.4 The handoff test — when CH2 tapers

**Do not taper on a date. Taper on this:**

> Inbound RFQs (CH3) exceed founder-generated RFQs (CH2) for **three consecutive months.**

Until then CH2 stays funded and staffed. §12.1 budgets MAD 2,000/month for field costs; if CH2 is running heavily in m8–12 that line is probably light.

### 19.5 CH3 — SEO and organic

Strategy, page architecture and the aged-domain question are in [§7.3.2](07-go-to-market.md). Operationally:

**Build order** (matches §8.3 item 4 — build early, iterate constantly):

1. **`/entreprise/{slug}`** — supplier profiles. Ship with the first 25 suppliers. Every onboarded supplier is a page
2. **`/fournisseurs/{secteur}/{ville}`** — the programmatic core. Only publish a page when it has **≥3 verified suppliers**. A thin page is worse than no page: it ranks badly, and a buyer who lands on an empty result never returns
3. **`/guides/{topic}`** — buyer education. Doubles as CH2 collateral (§7.3.2)
4. **`/prix/{materiau}/{region}`** — Phase 2+, once quote volume is statistically meaningful

**The thin-page rule is the one most likely to be broken under pressure.** The temptation at m6 is to publish the full city × category matrix for coverage. Resist it.

**Content cadence:** 2–4 guides/month, FR primary, AR for supplier-facing surfaces. §12.1 budgets MAD 2,000/month for freelance FR/AR writing.

**Measurement:** §10.4 targets **8,000 organic sessions/month by m12**. Watch **RFQs from organic**, not sessions — traffic that doesn't convert to RFQs means the pages rank for the wrong intent.

### 19.6 What we measure, and the one number that matters

| Channel | Leading indicator | Lagging | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| CH1 | Booked meetings/week | Verified suppliers | 100 by m12 |
| CH2 | Buyer conversations/week | RFQs submitted/month | 80 by m12 |
| CH3 | Pages indexed, impressions | Organic sessions | 8,000/mo by m12 |
| **All** | — | **Quote-to-deal conversion** | **>15% (Gate 2)** |

**Quote-to-deal conversion is the gate metric for Phase 2 (§15.3) and the single most important number in the business (§6.4).** Every channel above is upstream of it. A channel that delivers volume but not deals is a channel making things worse — more supplier attention spent, no supplier revenue earned.

**Instrument before launching any channel.** §8.4 principle 3: if a decision gate depends on a metric, the metric ships before the feature. **You cannot retrofit attribution onto conversations that already happened.**

Minimum tracking from day one: source of every supplier and buyer, date of first contact, channel, and — for suppliers — whether they were interviewed in Phase A. That last field tells you whether the warm list actually outperformed cold, which determines how much Phase A interviewing is worth in the second region.

### 19.7 The capacity problem nobody costs

**§11.1 gives the Growth founder all three channels, plus SEO architecture, plus funding applications, plus pricing.** In months 8–12 that means running CH1's tail, CH2 at full intensity, and CH3's content programme simultaneously.

**That is not a part-time job and it is arguably not a one-person job.**

Three honest options:

1. **Sequence hard.** Let CH1 taper genuinely before CH2 peaks. Risky: suppliers onboarded at m4 need demand by m7 or they churn
2. **Hire earlier than §11.2 plans.** It currently puts field sales/supplier success at m9–12. **Pulling it to m7–8 relieves exactly the crunch** — and if Tamwilcom's non-dilutive instruments land (§13.2), this is affordable sooner than v0.1 assumed
3. **The Product founder takes CH2.** They are building the RFQ router; routing the first fifty by hand is the best possible spec-gathering. §11.1 says "both talk to users" — this is where that stops being a platitude

**Recommendation: option 3 for months 6–10, option 2 from month 8.** Manual routing is product work as much as sales work, and the person building the matcher should feel the matching problem directly.

**❓ DECISION:** who owns CH2 in months 6–12? Decide before m6, not during it.

### 19.8 What would tell us a channel is wrong

Stated in advance, so we notice rather than rationalise:

| Signal | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| CH1 conversion <10% after 50 calls | The pitch or the target is wrong | Rewrite from interview quotes; check the region/vertical |
| Suppliers sign up but don't complete verification | Verification is too heavy, or the value is unclear | Re-examine §10.1 tiering |
| CH2 gets RFQs but <3 quotes each | Supply is too thin in those categories | Stop soliciting demand there; go back to CH1 |
| Suppliers receive RFQs but don't quote | Wrong suppliers, bad matching, or leads look unqualified | This is R4 forming. **Investigate immediately** |
| Quotes happen but deals don't | **The core loop is broken** | **Gate 2 fails. Fix before any escrow work (§15.3)** |
| CH3 traffic rises, RFQs flat | Ranking for the wrong intent | Re-examine page targeting, not volume |

**The fourth and fifth rows are R4** — §14.2's "quiet one," the risk that lets a platform look healthy while the model fails underneath. **These are the signals to watch weekly, not quarterly.**
